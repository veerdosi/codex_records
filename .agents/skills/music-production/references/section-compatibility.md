# Plan song pairings and handoffs

Score the actual passages that will meet: a particular hook, verse, backing section, or transition. Keep the source timestamps and intended transformation with the comparison. Two songs can fit in one passage and fight in another. A track-wide BPM or key is a starting point.

| Check | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Tempo | Beat timestamps and a local BPM fit. Resolve half-time and double-time readings against the actual drum accents. |
| Meter | Beats per bar and which beats carry the kick, snare, or other accents. |
| Downbeats | Bar-one timestamps in both sources and the planned destination. A two-beat shift in 4/4 can pass a beat-grid check and still put an entrance on the wrong bar accent. |
| Structure | Hook, verse, intro, outro, and phrase boundaries, including pickups, last syllables, and the bars that carry a handoff in the reference. |
| Key | Overall and local tonal center; confirm against bass and held notes in the sections used. |
| Chords | Time-aligned chord and bass movement at the join. Treat weak model labels as hypotheses. |
| Harmonic fit | Chroma and bass comparison after the proposed transposition; check sustained overlaps for clashes. |
| Rhythmic fit | Onsets, subdivisions, swing, syncopation, and cadence. Compare where stressed syllables fall against the new drum pattern. |
| Energy and timbre | Active-section loudness, spectral balance, arrangement density, and apparent pace. Check vocal and backing levels separately, including the speech band when they compete. |

1. Find phrase and downbeat anchors in both sources. Record any bar offset, tempo change, or transposition. Keep the vocal at source speed when cadence is a priority and audition any noticeable stretch against the original.
2. Rank a few section pairs under those transformations. Render short, level-matched previews that include the whole handoff. If a candidate needs a large speed change or a reset at every join, try another section or pairing.
3. Choose the transition in context. For a difficult handoff, use the [production reference library](../../../../work/shared/production_references/README.md) to find a recording that solves a similar problem.

Use Beat This or REAPER transients for timing and REAPER chord analysis or chroma for harmony. Use these readings to narrow the candidates before judging the preview.

## Build a handoff

- Give the intro, hook, verses, and ending distinct jobs. Check what the source arrangement carries across a phrase boundary, such as a drum pattern, held chord, or instrumental response. Keep complete consonants, breaths, and tails at vocal edits.
- If a bridge feels slow at the intended tempo, check the kick and backbeat on both sides. Try a pickup under the last outgoing phrase, then let the new groove take over at the incoming phrase boundary.
- When a chord return feels sudden, mark the outgoing ending and the intended full-level arrival. Bring the pitched layer in earlier while the drum pulse carries through. Check the bass entrance for clashes and level jumps.

After assembly, use the [rendered-mix review](rendered-mix-review.md) to check what the transition does to the pulse, balance, and phrasing.

## When to use a left–right handoff

- Use it when the arrangement gives the movement a job. A drum fill, synth answer, or delay repeat can move on a clear beat or subdivision and hand off to the next section. A short move can make the return to full width feel bigger. Choose its length and depth to fit the phrase. [Ableton’s Auto Pan-Tremolo reference](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/live-audio-effect-reference/#auto-pan-tremolo) · [iZotope on stereo contrast](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/6-tips-for-widening-the-stereo-image-of-a-mix)
- Keep the pulse, low end, and lead intelligible in both ears. Let the kick, bass, and lead anchor the center while a supporting element moves. The pan mode and pan law can change the level or attenuate one side of an already-stereo stem. Check the actual render before compensating. [Ableton’s panning and pan-law notes](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/audio-fact-sheet/#panning) · [iZotope on panning](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/what-is-panning-in-music)
- Level-match the moving and stationary versions. Check the whole handoff on headphones, on speakers, and in a mono sum. Listen for an unintended dip, a disappearing accent, or a jump when the full stereo image returns. Shorten or narrow the move if it interrupts the groove. Keep it only if the transition feels better.
